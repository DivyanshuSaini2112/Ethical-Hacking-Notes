## What is Cyber Law?

**Cyber Law** (also called IT Law) is the law regarding **Information Technology** including computers and the internet.

- Related to legal informatics
- Supervises digital circulation of **information, software, information security, and e-commerce**
- Does NOT constitute a separate area of law — encloses aspects of **contract, intellectual property, privacy, and data protection** laws
- **Intellectual property** is a key element of IT law

> **Ministry of Electronics and IT, Government of India:**
> *"Cyber Laws yields legal recognition to electronic documents and a structure to support e-filing and e-commerce transactions and also provides a legal structure to reduce, check cyber crimes."*

---

## Importance of Cyber Law

1. **Covers all transactions over the internet**
2. **Keeps eye on all activities over the internet**
3. **Touches every action and every reaction in cyberspace**

## IT Act 2000 - Background

**The Information Technology Act, 2000** was introduced when India was on the brink of digitalization.

**Core objective:** Providing legal recognition for **electronic data interchange** and other means of electronic communication.

**Based on:** UNCITRAL (United Nations Commission on International Trade Law) model

**Amended by:** Information Technology (Amendment) Act, **2008**

---

## IT Act 2000 - Objectives

The Act seeks to:
- ✅ Accord legal recognition to **E-Transactions** and **digital signatures** for authentication
- ✅ Make way for **electronic filing** of data and information
- ✅ Facilitate **electronic storage** of data
- ✅ Grant recognition for maintenance of books of accounts in **Electronic Form**
- ✅ **Levy offences** for defaults

---

## IT Amendment Act 2008 - Key Changes

The 2008 amendment:
- Laid emphasis on **data privacy and information security**
- Defined **cyber-café**
- Classified digital signatures as **technology neutral**
- Defined the security process to be followed by a **corporate**
- Redefined the role of **intermediaries**
- Recognized the role of **Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In)**
- Made way for inclusion of crimes such as **child pornography and cyber terrorism**
- Vested an **Inspector** (previously DSP) with rights of investigating cyber-crimes

## Key Sections of IT Act 2000

### Section 43 – Penalty for Damages
Any person **damaging the computer** (or its network) of the owner would be forced to remit a **penalty and compensation** to the person affected.

---

### Section 44 – Documentation and Reporting

| Violation | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Not furnishing required documents/returns | Up to **INR 1,50,000** |
| Failing to furnish information within time | **INR 5,000 per day** |
| Not maintaining books of accounts | **INR 10,000 per day** |

---

### Section 65 – Manipulating Source Document
Deliberately tampering, destroying, concealing, or altering any computer source document → **INR 2,00,000** and/or imprisonment up to **3 years**

---

### Section 66 – Dishonesty and Fraudulence
Committing dishonesty/fraudulence under Section 43 → **INR 5,00,000** and/or imprisonment up to **3 years**

**Sub-sections:**

| Section | Offence | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| **66B** | Dishonestly receiving/retaining stolen computer resource | INR 1,00,000 and/or 3 years |
| **66C** | Fraudulently using electronic signature/password/ID of another person | INR 1,00,000 and/or 3 years |
| **66D** | Dishonestly personating through communication device | INR 1,00,000 and/or 3 years |
| **66E** | Capturing/publishing private area image without consent | INR 2,00,000 and/or 3 years |
| **66F** | Threatening unity/integrity/security/sovereignty electronically | **Life imprisonment** |

---

### Section 67 – Electronic Publishing or Transmission
Transmitting material that appeals to **unhealthy interest** → **INR 5,00,000** and/or 3 years imprisonment.

**On conviction again:** INR 10,00,000 and/or 5 years.

**Section 67A:** Publishing sexually explicit content → INR 10,00,000 and/or 5 years. Repeated offences → up to **7 years**.

---

### Section 68 – Powers of the Controller
Controller may order Certifying Authority to cease activities. Contravening = **INR 1,00,000** and/or **2 years**.

---

### Section 69 – Powers of the Government
Government may order monitoring/decryption of information in national interest. Non-compliance → **7 years** imprisonment + fine.

---

### Section 70 – Declaration of Protected System
Non-compliance with protected critical infrastructure designation → **10 years** + fine.

---

### Section 71 – Misrepresentation or Suppression
Misrepresenting info to obtain License/Electronic Signature Certificate → **INR 1,00,000** and/or **2 years**.

---

### Section 72 – Provisions of Secured Access
Revealing secured electronic records without consent → **INR 1,00,000** and/or **2 years**.

If done with intent of wrongful gain → **INR 5,00,000** and/or **3 years**.

---

### Section 73 & 74 – Publishing of Certificate
- **73:** Publishing unauthorized Electronic Signature Certificate → **INR 1,00,000** and/or **2 years**
- **74:** Creating certificate for fraudulent purpose → **INR 1,00,000** and/or **2 years**

---

### Section 75 – Offence Outside India
Committing cyber offence involving Indian computer/network from **outside India** → still subject to Act penalties (regardless of nationality).

---

### Section 76 – Provision of Confiscation
Non-compliance with computer-related provisions → **confiscation of hardware**.

---

## Advantages of Cyber Law

1. Organizations can carry out **e-commerce** using legal infrastructure
2. **Digital signatures** have legal validity
3. Entry of corporate companies as **Certifying Authorities** for Digital Signatures
4. Government can issue notifications on web — **e-governance**
5. Companies can **file forms/applications** electronically with government
6. Addresses **security** issues critical to electronic transactions
7. Provides **both hardware and software security**
